# What a Fed Rate Cut Actually Does to Your Portfolio

The Fed is signaling cuts. Here is what that really means for stocks, bonds and your cash — in plain English.

## What is a rate cut?
When the Federal Reserve "cuts rates," it lowers the **federal funds rate** — the interest banks charge each other overnight [1]. That single number ripples into mortgages, credit cards, savings, and ultimately stock prices.

## Why markets care
Lower rates make borrowing cheaper and make safe yields on cash and bonds less attractive, pushing money toward riskier assets like stocks and raising the present value of future earnings [2]. Growth and tech names are usually most sensitive.

- **Stocks:** often a tailwind, especially rate-sensitive sectors.
- **Bonds:** existing higher-coupon bonds rise in price.
- **Cash:** money-market and savings yields drift lower.

## The catch for beginners
A cut is not an automatic "buy" signal. *Why* the Fed is cutting matters: easing into a healthy economy differs greatly from cutting because growth is collapsing [3]. Watch the reason, not just the headline.
